# Joint Build Budget Planner

Offline, read-only, Shadow. It answers one question: for a given race and the Stamina that race
demands, which parent pair, support deck and borrow best pay for that Stamina, and what does paying
for it cost in Speed, Power, Guts, Wit, skill points and deck slots.

Nothing in it is read by the bot at runtime. No training choice, lesson purchase, skill buy, deck
selection, borrow selection or career launch depends on any of it.

Code lives in `src/lib/buildBudget/`. The CLI is `scripts/build-budget.mjs`. The decoded evidence is
`src/data/build_budget_data.json`, generated by `scripts/generate-build-budget-data.mjs`.

## What it joins

| Input | Owner | What the planner takes |
|---|---|---|
| Survival requirement | `src/lib/raceSurvival/` (STAM-1) | `SurvivalConstraint`: minimum Stamina, preferred range, the recovery the minimum assumes, the risk policy, the confidence |
| Parent pairs | `src/lib/parentLab/` | Ranked pairs, and the parents' full Spark sets read off their Veteran evidence rather than off the pair's priority-filtered coverage |
| Decks | `src/lib/deckLab/` | Scored decks, their card value profiles and every decoded support-card effect value |
| Inheritance, growth, base training, stat caps, career length | `src/data/build_budget_data.json` | Decoded from `master.mdb` |

The planner defines no second vocabulary for distance, surface, running style or training stat. It
reuses ParentLab's and DeckLab's.

## What the generator decodes

`scripts/generate-build-budget-data.mjs` reads an installed `master.mdb` and re-proves its mapping on
every run. Everything below is `DECODED_GAME_DATA`.

| Fact | Source table | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Per-Spark effect ladder (start stat, stat cap, aptitude step, granted skill) | `succession_factor`, `succession_factor_effect` | VERIFIED |
| Spark names, per family | `text_data` category 147 | VERIFIED |
| Trainee growth percentages, per outfit | `card_data` `talent_*` | VERIFIED, asserted to sum to 30 |
| Trainee starting stats and aptitude letters, per star level | `card_rarity_data` | VERIFIED |
| Shared stat ceiling (1200) | `card_rarity_data` `max_*`, asserted uniform | VERIFIED |
| Per-scenario stat cap bonus | `single_mode_scenario` `max_*` | VERIFIED |
| Base training outcome per scenario, including secondary stats, energy and skill points | `single_mode_training_effect` | VERIFIED |
| Training failure rate per facility level | `single_mode_training` | VERIFIED |
| Career length per scenario (78 turns) | `single_mode_turn` | VERIFIED |

The one mapping worth knowing by heart, because it is not the obvious one: on the ordinary training
board `command_id` 101 is Speed, **102 is Power**, 103 is Guts, **105 is Stamina** and 106 is Wit,
while the camp board runs 601 to 605 straight down Speed, Stamina, Power, Guts, Wit. The generator
derives both from the effect rows and asserts each set raises five distinct stats; it never declares
them.

## The Spark ladder, and the one thing about it that is not decoded

`succession_factor_effect` is keyed by `(factor_group_id, effect_id)`. `effect_id` is a level, and the
ladder length differs per family: blue stat Sparks run to ten rungs, white skill Sparks to five,
unique Sparks to four, pink aptitude Sparks to two, and race and scenario Sparks to exactly three.

**How a lineage's star counts add up to a level on that ladder is not in `master.mdb.`** Whether a
grandparent's star weighs the same as a parent's, and whether the sum is taken before or after
clamping, is stated nowhere. So inheritance is priced as a bracket between two defensible readings:

- **low** only the strongest single carrier of a Spark counts,
- **high** every carrier's stars sum, clamped to that Spark's own ladder.

The truth is between them and the report says so. No middle rule is invented.

## The projection is a floor

Every income source the model declines to price is non-negative:

- the training facility level, which is not decoded (`single_mode_training_effect` is keyed by command,
  not by the 1..5 level the same command carries in `single_mode_training`),
- support-card and trainee events, whose stat payouts live in tables this planner does not read,
- scenario systems on top of the decoded base gains (Grand Concert lessons, Trackblazer gimmicks),
- the gated multipliers, which compound further than the single pass the model applies.

Nothing unpriced takes stats away. So a projection reads LOW against a finished career's stat screen,
and a survival verdict off it is conservative in the same direction. Compare candidates through these
figures; do not compare the figures to the game.

## The bracket

Every training projection is a range, and the split is on whether a term is gated:

- **low** the unconditional card terms only: the flat stat bonus a card adds to a training, and its
  training effectiveness. Both are paid whenever the card is on the training at all.
- **high** the gated terms on top: friendship, which pays nothing until the bond gauge is full, and
  mood, which pays nothing unless mood is kept up.

A floor that assumed the deck contributed nothing at all would not be a floor; it would be a build
with no deck.

## Verdict tiers

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| clears the floor | the LOW end of the Stamina projection clears the constraint minimum |
| marginal | the midpoint clears it, the low end does not |
| deficit | neither end clears it |
| unsatisfiable | the survival model reports no Stamina survives this race under this risk policy |

Deficit is measured at the low end and surplus at the median, on purpose: a build clears a floor when
even the pessimistic reading clears it, whereas over-investment is a question about the ordinary case
and would never fire off the low end.

## Flex archetypes

Three ways to pay for the Stamina, all evaluated against the same requirement and none gated by
trainee name:

- `STAMINA_FLEX` at least one dedicated Stamina support card, and Stamina turns.
- `POWER_FLEX` no Stamina support card; leans on Power training's decoded secondary Stamina.
- `NO_STAMINA_FLEX` no Stamina support card and no Stamina turns at all.

The Power-flex premise is a table, not a theory: Power training's own row carries a Stamina gain, and
it differs per scenario (URA Finale pays 6, Grand Concert pays 4).

DeckLab ranks decks by quality and is composition-blind, so its shortlist can be entirely
Stamina-carrying decks. The CLI therefore runs a second deck search over the same owned inventory with
Stamina-typed cards removed, so the no-Stamina archetypes have something to be evaluated on.

## Recovery access

STAM-1's minimum is conditional: it holds only if the recovery it assumed actually fires. The planner
checks whether the build can reach those skills at all, through inheritance (a Spark grants them),
a deck hint (available, but costs skill points) or the trainee's own list. A skill reachable by none
of those invalidates the minimum, and the planner either re-solves against a no-recovery constraint or
refuses to evaluate the build. "The card exists somewhere in the game" is not access.

## Editorial inputs

Two, both labelled everywhere they are used and neither a game figure:

- the **training-turn budget** (default 40 of the decoded 78 career turns). How many turns become
  trainings rather than races, rest and events is a run-time outcome, not a table.
- the **archetype turn profiles**, the weights that spread those turns across the five facilities.

## Telemetry boundary

STAM-2 consumes no telemetry. Where a later phase's measurements would enter is named in
`CALIBRATION_POINTS` in `src/lib/buildBudget/types.ts`, and nowhere else. The three evidence channels
(decoded mechanics, external reference, editorial model) are kept apart and never blended.

## Regenerating the data asset

```bash
node scripts/generate-build-budget-data.mjs --db "<path>/master.mdb"
node scripts/generate-build-budget-data.mjs --db "<path>/master.mdb" --check
node --test scripts/generate-build-budget-data.test.mjs
```
